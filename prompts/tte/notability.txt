IS THIS ENTITY WORTH A RECORD?

A new record is only warranted for a NOTABLE entity: one that is prominent or
distinguished, with significant coverage in reliable sources independent of the
subject. Trivial or incidental coverage is not enough, and being famous is not
the same as being notable. Appearing once in a news article is, by itself,
weak evidence.

A person is likely notable if they have received a well-known award or honour,
made a contribution that forms part of the enduring historical record, held
national or international office, are influential academically, are a creative
professional widely regarded or widely cited, competed at a major sporting
level, or had a significant role in a well-documented historic event.

An organisation is likely notable if it is of cultural, heritage or national
importance, has received significant awards, or has made a widely recognised
and enduring contribution.

A place is likely notable if it has historical or architectural significance,
holds protected or heritage status, or is a named natural feature.

Someone quoted in passing, a company mentioned only as an employer, or an
official named only for making an announcement is USUALLY NOT notable. Where
notability is doubtful, say so rather than proposing a new record.
