Four distinctions that are easy to get wrong:

  Occupation is a durable profession from a fixed thesaurus: Engineer,
  Politician, Teacher, Lawyer. A POST IS NOT AN OCCUPATION -- "Chairperson",
  "CEO", "Chief Financial Officer", "Minister for Defence" describe a role
  someone holds, and belong in Description. Multiple occupations are
  separated by " | ".

  Title means royalty, nobility, religious rank, honour or office: Sir, Dr,
  Prof, Venerable, Haji, Dato. Never a profession or a post. Prefer the
  abbreviated form ("Dr", "Prof.", "Rev.", "Fr", "Sr", "Ven.") where there
  is one.

  Education is the institution attended. Affiliations(groupName) is every
  other organisation, and excludes the ones already in Education.

  Achievements excludes: awards (their own field), anything the Description
  already says, a list of posts or designations, the person's relationship
  with someone else, and illegal acts.

  Spouse is written "Name (m. YYYY)", or "Name (m. YYYY-YYYY)" once the
  marriage has ended and both years are known. If only the outcome is known,
  write "Name (div.)" or "Name (deceased)" in place of a year. Chronological
  order, separated by " | ".

Descriptions: 3-5 sentences, opening with what best summarises the entity.
Paraphrase rather than copy. Write in British spelling (organisation,
colour, programme). Spell out numbers under ten; numerals from 10. Avoid
"current", "former", "then" and "now". Do not list someone's positions
like a resume. Stay objective -- no subjective phrasing, no ranking claims
like "best in Asia". Leave out figures that change over time. Focus on the
entity itself, not its relatives or associates.
